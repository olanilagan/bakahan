One cow can be a year of savings. If it dies from disease, a flood or a lightning strike, a small raiser can lose more than he can recover. The Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation (PCIC), a government agency, offers livestock insurance. Details change, so use this guide to know what to ask, and confirm the current terms at your nearest PCIC office.

## What it covers

PCIC's livestock insurance is mortality insurance. It pays when an insured animal dies. Cattle and carabao fall under the program, along with other animals such as swine, horses, goats and sheep. The exact list of covered causes of death, and the exclusions, are in the policy. Ask PCIC for a copy and read the section on what is not covered, such as deaths from neglect.

Most insurance does not pay if an animal is simply sold at a loss or loses weight. Check the wording.

## Who can apply and for which animals

According to a PCIC program profile, cattle and carabao are insurable from 7 months to 5 years of age. Animals over 5 years but under 17 may be accepted for an additional premium. These details may have been updated, so confirm them.

The profile lists two kinds of cover: non-commercial and commercial. The non-commercial cover is aimed at small raisers, and the commercial cover is for larger operations. Ask which one applies to your herd.

## What it costs

A PCIC guideline lists premium rates for non-commercial cattle and carabao cover of 5.0 to 6.5 percent of the sum insured, depending on the sum insured. The 2020 profile lists commercial cover at 5 to 10 percent and above. The guidelines say rates can change, and the sources date from 2016 and 2020, so ask PCIC for today's rate table.

An example, using made-up round numbers: if a fattener is insured for ₱30,000 and the rate is 5 percent, the premium would be ₱1,500. Your actual rate and amount will be different.

## Free coverage for some farmers

PCIC can subsidize premiums for farmers listed in the Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA). A ReliefWeb article said free coverage for marginal farmers was offered through 2022, with a limit of 10 head for cattle, carabao and horses. Whether this still applies depends on the current program and funding. Register in the RSBSA through your MAO, because many government programs, including loans, start from that list.

## How to apply

The steps differ a little by region, but the usual order is:

- Go to the PCIC regional or provincial office, or ask your MAO to connect you with PCIC.
- Bring a valid ID, your RSBSA registration if you have one, and the details of your animals.
- The animals are inspected and identified, often with a tag or other marks. Ask what PCIC requires.
- You choose a sum insured within the allowed range and pay the premium unless it is subsidized.
- You receive a policy. Keep it with your records.

## Make a claim that will be accepted

Claims are rejected most often for paperwork reasons. Based on what insurers commonly require, and on what PCIC tells you, plan on the following. Ask PCIC for its exact claim rules, including the notice deadline, because the notice period and document list were not confirmed for this guide.

- Report the loss quickly. Insurers usually set a tight deadline, sometimes only days, and a late report can void the claim. Ask PCIC what the period is and save the phone number where you can find it.
- Do not bury or dispose of the carcass until PCIC or a veterinarian has seen it, unless the disease risk requires it. Ask them what to do.
- Keep the ear tag or identification.
- Take clear photos of the animal and the tag.
- Ask the veterinarian for a written statement of the cause of death.
- Keep the policy and receipts.

If the death looks like a disease that must be reported, such as one with blisters in the mouth and feet, tell the veterinarian and BAI first.

## Insurance and loans

Lenders sometimes want insurance. The DA Ilocos cattle loan described in the business guide came with livestock insurance through PCIC and required training. If you plan to borrow for cattle, ask whether PCIC cover is needed and add the premium to your budget.

## Is it worth it

For a small raiser, the premium is a fixed cost that comes out of the margin. Insurance does not prevent losses, so good care still comes first. But for a larger herd, or for a first-time raiser whose savings are in one or two animals, protection against a sudden loss can be worth the cost. Do the math with your own numbers, compare it to the money you could lose, and decide.

## Summary

- PCIC livestock insurance pays when an insured animal dies, according to the policy terms. Ask for the full list of covered causes and exclusions.
- Older PCIC documents list cattle as insurable from 7 months to 5 years, at premiums of about 5 to 6.5 percent for non-commercial cover. Confirm current rates.
- RSBSA-registered farmers may qualify for subsidized or free premiums, depending on the current program.
- Report any loss quickly, keep the tag, take photos and get a vet statement.
- Add the premium to your cost computation and compare it with the loss you could not afford.
